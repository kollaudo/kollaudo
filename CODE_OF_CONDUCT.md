# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want Kollaudo to be a project where anyone can take part and feel respected, whatever their
age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion or sexual identity and orientation.

We promise to act and interact in ways that keep the community open, welcoming and healthy.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that helps the community:

- Being kind and patient with people who are new to the project or to the tools it works with.
- Respecting other points of view and experiences.
- Giving feedback that is specific and useful, and accepting it with grace.
- Taking responsibility for our mistakes and learning from them.
- Thinking about what is best for the community as a whole and not only for ourselves.

Examples of behavior that is not acceptable:

- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as a physical or email address, without
  their permission.
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in every space of the project: the repositories under
[github.com/kollaudo](https://github.com/kollaudo) (issues, pull requests, discussions and
reviews) and any other place where someone represents the project in public, such as a talk or a
post made on its behalf.

## Enforcement responsibilities

The maintainers explain what acceptable behavior is and take fair action when it is not followed.
They can remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that do
not follow this Code of Conduct. When they do, they say why.

## Reporting

To report unacceptable behavior, write to [conduct@kollaudo.dev](mailto:conduct@kollaudo.dev).

All reports are reviewed and answered. The maintainers keep the identity of the person who reported
private, and the maintainers must not take part in a decision about a report that involves them.

## Enforcement guidelines

The maintainers follow these steps when they decide what to do about a violation.

1. **Correction.** For a single inappropriate comment or a similar small lapse. The response is a
   private message that explains what was wrong and why. An apology may be asked for.
2. **Warning.** For a violation that continues or happens more than once. The response is a warning
   with consequences if it happens again, and no contact with the people involved for a period of
   time. This includes not interacting with them in the project's spaces.
3. **Temporary ban.** For a serious violation or sustained inappropriate behavior. The person may
   not interact or communicate with the community for a period of time. Breaking these terms can
   lead to a permanent ban.
4. **Permanent ban.** For a pattern of violations, harassment of a person or aggression towards
   groups of people. The person may never again take part in the community.
